# Board and Executive Templates

**Verify before reliance.** Committee mandates and thresholds (for example which listed entities must have a Risk Management Committee) change by amendment. Search the official source (mca.gov.in, sebi.gov.in, rbi.org.in, irdai.gov.in) and record the date checked.

## 1. Who oversees cyber, data and AI risk in India (indicative)

| Body | Source (verify current text) | Relevance to cyber / data / AI |
|---|---|---|
| Board of Directors | Companies Act 2013, s.166 (duties: act in good faith, with due and reasonable care, skill and diligence); s.134(5) directors' responsibility statement (internal financial controls for listed companies; proper systems to ensure compliance with all applicable laws); s.134(3)(n) risk management policy statement in the Board report | Ultimate accountability; approves cyber and information security policy; accepts residual risk above appetite |
| Audit Committee | Companies Act s.177; SEBI LODR Reg. 18 | Internal financial controls, IS audit findings, internal audit coverage of IT |
| Risk Management Committee | SEBI LODR Reg. 21 and Schedule II Part D (role includes cyber security; applies to the top 1,000 listed entities by market capitalisation at the end of the previous financial year; verify the current threshold) | Risk framework, cyber risk oversight, meets at least twice a year |
| IT Strategy Committee | RBI Master Direction on IT Governance, Risk, Controls and Assurance Practices (07.11.2023) | Board-level oversight of IT and cyber risk for regulated entities |
| Board / committee under SEBI CSCRF | SEBI CSCRF (20.08.2024) | Board-approved cyber security and resilience policy; oversight of CSCRF compliance |
| Board of an insurer | IRDAI Information and Cyber Security Guidelines (2023) | Board-approved policy; information security committee |
| Data Protection Officer reporting line | DPDP Act s.10 (Significant Data Fiduciary) | DPO based in India, responsible to the Board of Directors or similar governing body |

**Global benchmarks:** NIST CSF 2.0 Govern function; ISO/IEC 27014 (governance of information security); ISO/IEC 38500 (governance of IT); ISO/IEC 38507 (governance implications of AI); NIS2 Art. 20 (management bodies approve and oversee cyber risk measures, and must be trained); WEF / NACD principles for board cyber oversight.

## 2. Board / committee memo template

> **To:** [Board / Committee] **From:** [CISO / CRO / DPO / CAIO] **Date:** [dd.mm.yyyy]
> **Subject:** [Topic] — **For: Decision / Approval / Assurance / Information**

1. **Purpose and ask** (2–3 lines). "The Committee is asked to approve X / note Y / accept residual risk Z until [date]."
2. **Headline.** Overall position in one sentence, with trend (improving / stable / worsening) and the reason.
3. **Context.** What triggered this paper (regulatory change, incident, audit, strategy), in 3–5 bullets.
4. **Analysis.** The top risks or issues against appetite, in a table: issue → business impact → current rating → target → owner → date. Add a Basis column showing Observed / Assessed / Assumed.
5. **Options.** 2–4 options: cost, risk reduction, regulatory position, time to deliver, key dependencies.
6. **Recommendation** and the reason, in one paragraph.
7. **Legal and regulatory implications.** Obligations engaged, deadlines, reporting duties, penalty exposure band (cite and date-verify).
8. **Resources.** Budget (capex/opex), people, external support.
9. **Proposed resolution / minute wording.**
10. **Annexes.** Risk register extract, heat map, KRI table, glossary.

Keep to 2–4 pages. Put no unexplained acronym in the first page.

## 3. Draft resolution / minute wording (adapt)

- **Approval of policy:** "RESOLVED THAT the [Information and Cyber Security Policy, version x.y], as placed before the Board, be and is hereby approved, and that [CISO] be authorised to implement it and report compliance to the [Committee] [quarterly]."
- **Risk acceptance:** "The Committee noted the residual risk [ID, description], rated [rating] against an appetite of [level], and accepted it until [date], subject to the compensating controls [list] and a review at the [month] meeting. Accepting owner: [executive]."
- **Budget:** "RESOLVED THAT expenditure of up to ₹[amount] for [programme] be approved, to be incurred in [phases], with progress reported to the [Committee] each quarter."
- **Noting:** "The Committee noted the report on [incident / assessment] and directed management to [action] by [date]."

Confirm the wording with the company secretary; resolutions must fit the Articles and the committee's terms of reference.

## 4. Executive one-page brief

| Section | Content |
|---|---|
| **Bottom line** | One sentence: what is happening and what you need from the reader |
| **Why it matters to you** | The impact in the reader's terms (money, service, liability, people, reputation) |
| **Three facts** | Each tagged Observed / Assessed / Assumed |
| **Options** | Two or three, with cost and consequence |
| **Recommendation** | One option and why |
| **What we need from you** | Decision, budget, sponsorship, message, by [date] |
| **Next update** | When and through which channel |

## 5. Talking points and Q&A

- Five talking points maximum, each one sentence, no jargon.
- Prepare answers to likely questions: "Are we compliant?", "Could this happen to us?", "Has any customer data been affected?", "What does it cost?", "What are peers doing?", "What must we tell the regulator, and by when?", "Who is accountable?"
- Mark any answer resting on an Assumed fact, and do not let the executive state it as certain.

## 6. Decision note (internal, short)

1. Decision required and decision-maker.
2. Deadline and the cost of not deciding by it.
3. Options (table).
4. Recommendation.
5. Risks of the recommendation and how they will be managed.
6. Sign-off line.

## 7. Quality checklist before submission

- Does page one tell the reader what to do?
- Is every figure sourced and labelled?
- Are all deadlines absolute dates, with the legal source verified and dated?
- Is the ask within the body's authority under its terms of reference?
- Is the paper free of other clients' information and of any offensive technical detail?
- Has counsel or the company secretary reviewed the legal statements and resolution wording?
