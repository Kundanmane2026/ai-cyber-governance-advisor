# IT Act 2000 and IT Rules 2021

**Verify before reliance.** Check the amended text on indiacode.nic.in and the MeitY notifications. Punishment ranges below reflect the Act as amended in 2008 **and by the Jan Vishwas (Amendment of Provisions) Act 2023** (No. 18 of 2023; IT Act amendments in force from **30.11.2023**, notification S.O. 4745(E) dated 31.10.2023). Jan Vishwas decriminalised several provisions into civil penalties adjudicated under s.46: s.33(2), s.44, s.45 (now penalty up to ₹1 lakh plus compensation up to ₹10 lakh for an intermediary/company or ₹1 lakh for others), s.66A (omitted), s.67C(2), s.68(2), s.69B(4), s.70B(7), s.72 and s.72A. Older cases and textbooks show the pre-2023 punishments. BNS 2023 has replaced the IPC offences cited in older cases.

## 2. Landmark judgments

- **Shreya Singhal v. Union of India**, (2015) 5 SCC 1: s.66A struck down; s.69A upheld; s.79 "actual knowledge" read as a court order or government notification.
- **Justice K.S. Puttaswamy v. Union of India**, (2017) 10 SCC 1: privacy is a fundamental right under Article 21, with a proportionality test. This is the foundation of the DPDP Act.
- **Anvar P.V. v. P.K. Basheer** (2014) and **Arjun Panditrao Khotkar** (2020): electronic evidence certificate (see `cyber-forensics-evidence`).
- **Kunal Kamra v. Union of India** (Bombay HC, 2024): the 2023 amendment to IT Rules Rule 3(1)(b)(v) creating a government Fact Check Unit was struck down. Verify any appeal status.

## 3. IT (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) Rules 2021

**Verify amendments:** 2022 (Grievance Appellate Committees), 2023 (online gaming; fact-check unit, later struck down), and MeitY amendments or advisories on synthetically generated / deepfake content (2024–2025). Check what is notified and in force.

### Rule 3: due diligence (all intermediaries)
- Publish the rules and regulations, privacy policy and user agreement; inform users not to host specified prohibited content.
- Remove or disable access to unlawful content within **36 hours** of a court order or government notification.
- Content exposing private areas, nudity, sexual acts or impersonation (including morphed images): remove within **24 hours** of a complaint.
- Retain information about removed content and user registration for **180 days** (or longer if required).
- Provide information or assistance to authorised government agencies within **72 hours** of an order.
- Report cyber security incidents to CERT-In.
- Grievance Officer: acknowledge within 24 hours; resolve within **15 days** (shorter for some categories, verify); users can appeal to a Grievance Appellate Committee.

### Rule 4: Significant Social Media Intermediaries (≥ 50 lakh registered users in India)
- Chief Compliance Officer, Nodal Contact Person and Resident Grievance Officer, all resident in India.
- Monthly compliance reports.
- Messaging services: identification of the **first originator** on a court or competent-authority order for specified offences. This is under constitutional challenge, so verify the status.
- Proactive technology-based measures for certain content (with safeguards).
- Voluntary user verification; notice to users when their content is removed.

### Part III: digital media ethics (publishers of news and OTT curated content)
Code of Ethics; three-tier grievance mechanism. Several provisions were stayed by High Courts (2021), so verify.
