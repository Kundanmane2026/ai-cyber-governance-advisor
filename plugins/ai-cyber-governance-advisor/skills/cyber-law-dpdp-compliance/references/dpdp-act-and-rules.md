# DPDP Act 2023 and DPDP Rules 2025

**Verify before reliance.** The DPDP Act 2023 (No. 22 of 2023; assent 11.08.2023) commences in stages through notifications. The DPDP Rules 2025 were notified in November 2025 with phased commencement. As understood when drafted:
- **Immediately:** the definitions and the Data Protection Board provisions.
- **About 12 months later:** Consent Manager registration.
- **About 18 months later (around May 2027):** most substantive obligations (notice, safeguards, breach, erasure, children, SDF, rights, cross-border).

Confirm the exact dates in the Gazette and on the MeitY site before advising, and state the commencement status for every obligation.

## 1. Act: section map

| Section | Subject | Key points |
|---|---|---|
| s.2 | Definitions | Data Fiduciary (determines purpose and means); Data Processor; Data Principal (includes parent/lawful guardian of a child and of a person with disability); personal data; **personal data breach** (s.2(u)); Consent Manager; child (under 18) |
| s.3 | Application | Digital personal data in India (collected digitally, or offline and digitised); outside India if connected with offering goods/services to Data Principals in India. Excludes personal/domestic use and data made publicly available by the Data Principal or under law |
| s.4 | Grounds | Lawful purpose with **consent** or for **legitimate uses** |
| **s.5** | Notice | Itemised personal data and purpose; manner of exercising rights; manner of complaining to the Board. Notice for consents taken before commencement "as soon as reasonably practicable". English or any Eighth Schedule language option |
| **s.6** | Consent | Free, specific, informed, unconditional, unambiguous, clear affirmative action, limited to necessary data; withdrawal as easy as giving; Consent Managers (s.6(7)–(9)) registered with the Board; burden of proof on the Fiduciary (s.6(10)) |
| **s.7** | Legitimate uses | Voluntarily provided data with no objection to use; State subsidies/services; legal obligations; court orders; medical emergency; epidemics; disasters; **employment purposes** (including preventing corporate espionage, protecting confidential information, providing benefits) |
| **s.8** | General obligations of a Data Fiduciary | Responsible even for processor actions (s.8(1)); processor only under a **valid contract** (s.8(2)); completeness/accuracy where used for decisions or disclosed (s.8(3)); technical and organisational measures (s.8(4)); **reasonable security safeguards** (s.8(5)); **breach intimation** to the Board and each affected Data Principal (s.8(6)); **erasure** when the purpose is no longer served or consent is withdrawn, including by the processor (s.8(7)–(8)); publish business contact / DPO (s.8(9)); grievance redressal (s.8(10)) |
| **s.9** | Children and persons with disability | **Verifiable parental consent**; no processing detrimental to the child's well-being; **no tracking, behavioural monitoring or targeted advertising directed at children**; exemptions by notification |
| **s.10** | Significant Data Fiduciary | Notified by the Central Government; **DPO based in India** responsible to the board; **independent data auditor**; periodic **DPIA**, periodic audit, other measures |
| s.11–14 | Data Principal rights | Information about processing (s.11); correction, completion, updating, erasure (s.12); grievance redressal (s.13; exhaust before approaching the Board); nominate (s.14) |
| s.15 | Duties of Data Principals | No impersonation, no false complaints, and so on. Penalty up to ₹10,000 |
| **s.16** | Cross-border transfer | Allowed **except** to countries restricted by Central Government notification (negative list); **s.16(2): sectoral laws with stricter rules prevail** |
| s.17 | Exemptions | Legal rights and claims, courts, crime prevention/investigation, outsourced processing of non-residents' data under a foreign contract, mergers, defaulters' financial data; State instrumentalities by notification; research/archiving/statistics (with standards); startups (notified classes) |
| s.18–26 | Data Protection Board of India | Digital office; inquiries; directions; urgent measures |
| s.27–28 | Board powers and procedure | Civil court powers for summons and inspection |
| s.29–31 | Appeals and ADR | Appeal to TDSAT within 60 days; mediation |
| s.32 | Voluntary undertaking | Accepted by the Board; bars proceedings; breach is penalised |
| **s.33** | Penalties | Per the **Schedule**; factors: nature, gravity, duration, type of data, repetition, gain/loss avoided, mitigation, proportionality, impact |
| s.37 | Blocking | Central Government may block on the Board's advice after repeated penalties |
| s.38 | Consistency with other laws | Additional to, not in derogation of, other laws; the DPDP Act prevails in conflict |
| s.44 | Amendments | **Omits IT Act s.43A**; amends the IT Act s.81 proviso; amends RTI Act s.8(1)(j) |

## 2. Penalty Schedule (s.33)

| # | Breach | Maximum penalty |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Failure to take reasonable security safeguards to prevent a personal data breach (s.8(5)) | **₹250 crore** |
| 2 | Failure to notify the Board or affected Data Principals of a breach (s.8(6)) | **₹200 crore** |
| 3 | Breach of additional obligations for children (s.9) | **₹200 crore** |
| 4 | Breach of additional SDF obligations (s.10) | **₹150 crore** |
| 5 | Breach of Data Principal duties (s.15) | ₹10,000 |
| 6 | Breach of a voluntary undertaking (s.32) | Up to the amount applicable for the underlying breach |
| 7 | Breach of any other provision of the Act or Rules | **₹50 crore** |

Penalties are civil, imposed by the Board after an inquiry. There are no criminal offences under DPDP itself.

## 3. DPDP Rules 2025: rule map (verify numbering and text)

| Rule | Subject | Key points |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | Notice | Standalone, clear, plain language; itemised description of data and specified purposes, with the goods/services enabled; communication link for withdrawing consent, exercising rights, and complaining to the Board |
| 4 + First Schedule | Consent Managers | Registration with the Board; company incorporated in India; net worth and technical/organisational conditions; interoperable platform; records of consents; conflict-of-interest safeguards |
| 5 + Second Schedule | Processing by the State | Standards for subsidies, benefits and services |
| **6** | **Reasonable security safeguards** | At minimum: encryption, obfuscation, masking or virtual tokens; access control; visibility through logs, monitoring and review; continued processing in a compromise (backups); **retain logs and personal data for one year** for detection and investigation (unless law requires otherwise); appropriate processor contract provisions; technical and organisational measures |
| **7** | **Breach intimation** | To each affected Data Principal without delay (nature, extent, timing, location; likely consequences; mitigation; safety measures; contact). To the Board without delay, then a **detailed report within 72 hours** (or a longer period allowed on request) |
| 8 + Third Schedule | Erasure timelines | For specified classes (large e-commerce, online gaming, social media intermediaries above user thresholds): erase after a set period (for example 3 years) of Data Principal inactivity, with **48 hours' notice** before erasure; retain logs for one year |
| 9 | Contact information | Publish the business contact information of the DPO or a person able to answer questions, prominently on the website/app and in every response |
| **10** | **Verifiable parental consent** | Due diligence that the person is an adult and the parent; use reliable identity and age details held by the Fiduciary, voluntarily provided details, or a virtual token from an authorised entity (for example DigiLocker) |
| 11 | Persons with disability | Verify the lawful guardian (court-appointed, or under the National Trust Act / RPwD Act) |
| 12 + Fourth Schedule | Exemptions from s.9 | Classes such as clinical establishments, educational institutions, crèches, and specified purposes (for example real-time location for safety, email accounts) |
| **13** | **SDF additional obligations** | **DPIA and audit once every 12 months**, with the significant observations reported to the Board; due diligence that algorithmic software is not likely to risk Data Principal rights; personal data and traffic data specified by the government (on a committee's recommendation) to be **processed in India** |
| 14 | Data Principal rights | Publish the means and identifiers needed; respond to grievances within **90 days** (verify); nomination |
| 15 | Cross-border transfer | Comply with the requirements the Central Government specifies by order for making data available to foreign States or their entities |
| 16 + Second Schedule | Research, archiving and statistics | Exemption if the standards are followed |
| 17–21 | Board | Composition, salary, procedure, digital office |
| 22 + Seventh Schedule | Calling for information | Central Government may call for information for specified purposes |
| 23 | Appeals | To the Appellate Tribunal (TDSAT) |

## 4. Children: practical checklist

- [ ] Age-gating at onboarding (declaration plus risk-based assurance)
- [ ] Verifiable parental consent route (Rule 10)
- [ ] No targeted ads, tracking or behavioural monitoring for under-18 users (unless exempt)
- [ ] Well-being assessment for features (no detrimental effect)
- [ ] Check whether a Fourth Schedule exemption applies, and document it

## 5. Significant Data Fiduciary readiness

- [ ] Self-assessment against the notification criteria (volume, sensitivity, risk to rights, sovereignty, public order, electoral democracy)
- [ ] DPO based in India, reporting to the board
- [ ] Independent data auditor appointed
- [ ] Annual DPIA and audit cycle; reporting to the Board
- [ ] Algorithmic due diligence (links to the `ai-governance-risk` skill)
- [ ] Data localisation for notified categories

## 6. Cross-border transfer analysis

1. Is the destination on a s.16 restricted list? (Search for the current notification.)
2. Does a sectoral law impose localisation or a stricter rule (RBI payment data, IRDAI, SEBI, telecom)? s.16(2) preserves it.
3. Is the entity an SDF with notified localisation categories (Rule 13)?
4. Are there contractual safeguards with the foreign recipient (processor clauses, security, onward transfer)?
5. Does a GDPR or other foreign transfer regime apply in the reverse direction (data coming *into* India from the EU)? See the overlay file.

## 7. Comparison: DPDP versus GDPR (headline)

| Topic | DPDP 2023 | GDPR |
|---|---|---|
| Scope | Digital personal data only | All personal data (automated or filing system) |
| Lawful bases | Consent + legitimate uses (closed list) | Six bases, including legitimate interests |
| Sensitive data | No separate category (sensitivity is a factor for SDFs) | Special categories (Art. 9) |
| Children | Under 18; parental consent; no tracking/targeted ads | Under 16 (member states may lower to 13) for ISS |
| Breach notice | Board + every affected Data Principal; 72 h detailed report | Authority within 72 h if risk; individuals if high risk |
| Transfers | Negative list | Adequacy / SCCs / BCRs and so on |
| Penalties | Up to ₹250 crore per instance | Up to €20m / 4% of global turnover |
| Rights | Information, correction, erasure, grievance, nomination | Plus portability, objection, automated decision-making |
| Publicly available data | Excluded if made public by the Data Principal or under law | Covered |
