# NDA Review Checklist and Restrictive Covenants

**Verify before reliance.** Confirm case citations and any recent High Court decisions on non-solicitation by search (main.sci.gov.in, High Court sites, indiankanoon.org for text only). State stamp duty under the applicable State Stamp Act. Record the date checked. Never cite a case you have not verified.

## 1. Clause-by-clause checklist

Grade each clause: **A** acceptable / **N** negotiate / **U** unacceptable.

| # | Clause | What to check | Preferred position (advisor as recipient) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Parties | Correct legal entities; group affiliates covered as disclosees? | Named entities; affiliates only if needed |
| 2 | Mutual or one-way | Matches the information flow | Mutual if both sides disclose |
| 3 | Purpose | Narrow, specific purpose | "Evaluating and performing [engagement]" |
| 4 | Definition of confidential information | Marked vs all information; oral disclosures confirmed in writing within [x] days | Reasonable definition; not "everything ever said" |
| 5 | Exclusions | Public domain; already known; independently developed; received from a third party without duty | All four standard exclusions present |
| 6 | Permitted disclosees | Employees, subcontractors, professional advisers, insurers on a need-to-know basis, bound by equivalent duties | Included |
| 7 | Standard of care | "Reasonable care", not less than for own information | Not absolute or "best efforts" |
| 8 | Compelled disclosure | Court, regulator, law enforcement, CERT-In directions, Data Protection Board, statutory audit | Carve-out with prompt notice where lawful; no clause that blocks mandatory reporting |
| 9 | Personal data | If personal data is shared: DPDP processor terms (instructions, safeguards, breach notice, erasure, sub-processors) | Separate DPA or schedule |
| 10 | Security requirements | Proportionate controls; no impossible obligations | Reference to the data-handling plan |
| 11 | Return and destruction | On request or at end; carve-out for backups, legal and professional retention | Carve-out present; certificate on request |
| 12 | Term and survival | Agreement term; duration of confidentiality after end | 2–5 years typical; trade secrets for as long as they remain secret |
| 13 | Residuals | Freedom to use general skills and knowledge retained in unaided memory | Acceptable for advisors; disclosers often resist |
| 14 | No licence / no obligation | No IP licence; no obligation to proceed | Present |
| 15 | Non-solicit / non-compete riders | Hidden restraints in an NDA | See section 2; usually remove non-compete |
| 16 | Remedies | Injunction; indemnity; liquidated damages | Injunction acceptable; resist uncapped indemnity and penal liquidated damages (s.74 allows only reasonable compensation) |
| 17 | Liability cap | Uncapped for breach of confidence? | Negotiate a cap or exclude consequential loss |
| 18 | Governing law, forum, dispute resolution | Indian law and seat; arbitration under the Arbitration and Conciliation Act 1996 if preferred | Clear and practical |
| 19 | Whistle-blowing and legal rights | Must not prevent lawful disclosures to authorities | Express carve-out |
| 20 | Execution and stamp duty | Authorised signatory; stamped under the State Stamp Act; e-stamping / e-signature (IT Act s.5 and Second Schedule) | Correct stamp duty paid before or at execution |

**Red flags:** blocks statutory reporting; unlimited indemnity; post-termination non-compete; one-way NDA where the advisor will also disclose methodology; perpetual obligations on all information; ownership of the advisor's pre-existing know-how; disclosure only with the discloser's consent, even to own staff.

## 2. Indian Contract Act s.27 (restraint of trade)

**Text (substance):** every agreement by which anyone is restrained from exercising a lawful profession, trade or business of any kind is void to that extent. The only statutory exception is for a seller of goodwill, who may agree not to carry on a similar business within specified local limits, if reasonable.

**Leading authorities (verify citations):**

| Case | Principle |
|---|---|
| *Niranjan Shankar Golikari v. Century Spinning and Mfg. Co.* (AIR 1967 SC 1098) | A negative covenant operating **during** the term of employment is not a restraint of trade under s.27 and can be enforced |
| *Superintendence Co. of India v. Krishan Murgai* ((1981) 2 SCC 246; AIR 1980 SC 1717) | A **post-termination** restraint on carrying on a trade or profession is void under s.27; the English reasonableness test does not apply (the leading statement is in A.P. Sen J.'s concurring opinion; the majority also decided on construction of the clause) |
| *Gujarat Bottling Co. v. Coca Cola Co.* ((1995) 5 SCC 545) | A negative stipulation during the subsistence of a franchise agreement is not void under s.27 |
| *Percept D'Mark (India) v. Zaheer Khan* ((2006) 4 SCC 227) | Restrictive clauses operating after the contract ends (right of first refusal/matching) are void under s.27 |

**Practical position (Assessed; confirm with counsel):**
- Post-employment or post-engagement **non-compete**: generally void and unenforceable.
- **Confidentiality** and trade-secret obligations: enforceable during and after the relationship, as they protect information rather than restrain trade.
- **Non-solicitation** of clients or employees after termination: courts have reached differing views; some High Courts treat it as a restraint, others enforce it where it protects confidential information. Verify recent decisions in the relevant High Court.
- **Garden leave** (paid, during the notice period): more likely enforceable as it operates during employment.
- A **foreign governing-law** clause may not save a post-termination restraint where enforcement is sought in India.

## 3. Remedies for breach of confidence

- Injunction (Specific Relief Act 1963; interim relief under the CPC Order XXXIX or s.9 of the Arbitration Act).
- Damages (Contract Act s.73) or reasonable compensation up to a stated sum (s.74).
- Delivery up or destruction of material.
- Statutory routes where facts fit: IT Act s.72A penalty and s.43 compensation before the Adjudicating Officer (s.46); criminal complaint only for offences such as IT Act s.66 or BNS criminal breach of trust (s.316). Seek counsel before making complaints.

## 4. Suggested wording (adapt)

- **Compelled disclosure:** "Nothing in this Agreement prevents the Recipient from disclosing Confidential Information to the extent required by law, regulation, court order, or a direction of a competent authority, including CERT-In or the Data Protection Board of India, provided that, where lawful, the Recipient gives the Discloser prompt notice and discloses only what is required."
- **Retention carve-out:** "The Recipient may retain copies required by law or professional standards, and copies in automatic backups, subject to continuing confidentiality until destroyed."
- **Residuals:** "Nothing restricts either party's use of general skills, know-how and experience retained in the unaided memory of its personnel, provided this does not permit disclosure of Confidential Information."
