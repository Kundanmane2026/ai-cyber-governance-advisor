# Professional Conduct and Public Servants

**Verify before reliance.** The CCS (Conduct) Rules 1964, Fundamental Rules, DoPT office memoranda, State conduct rules and professional codes are amended from time to time. Search dopt.gov.in, the State GAD site, barcouncilofindia.org, icai.org, isaca.org, isc2.org and cert-in.org.in, and record the date checked. Rule numbers below follow the Central rules; State rules differ.

## 1. Serving Central Government employees — CCS (Conduct) Rules 1964

| Rule | Substance (paraphrased; read the current text) | Effect on a private advisory engagement |
|---|---|---|
| **Rule 15 — Private trade or employment** | No Government servant shall, except with the **previous sanction** of the Government, engage directly or indirectly in any trade or business, negotiate for or undertake any other employment, or hold an elective office or canvass for business. Limited exceptions allow honorary work of a social or charitable nature, occasional work of a literary, artistic or scientific character, and certain sports or co-operative activity, subject to conditions (no conflict with official duties, report within the prescribed time, stop if directed). | Paid consulting, training, running a firm, or being a partner or director in a business needs **prior written sanction**. Sanction is sought before negotiating, not after. |
| **Rule 11 — Unauthorised communication of information** | A Government servant shall not communicate any official document or information, directly or indirectly, to any person not authorised to receive it, except in accordance with a general or special order or in good-faith performance of duties. | No use of official information, contacts or documents in private work, proposals or training material. |
| **Rule 13 — Gifts** | Limits on accepting gifts and hospitality; reporting thresholds | Applies to anything received from prospective clients |
| **Rule 16 — Investment, lending and borrowing** | Restrictions on speculative investment and on dealings with persons having official dealings | Check before taking equity in a client or start-up |
| **Rule 4 — Employment of near relatives** | Restrictions where relatives work for firms with official dealings | Disclose conflicts |
| **Rule 8 / 9** | Connection with the press; criticism of Government | Public writing or talks on government cyber policy |

Related: **FR 46** (fees and honoraria for work done with permission, including deposit of a share where prescribed); **Official Secrets Act 1923** (wrongful communication of official secrets); **Prevention of Corruption Act 1988**; post-retirement commercial employment rules (for example CCS (Pension) Rules for Group A officers — verify). **State Government servants** must check their State's Civil Services (Conduct) Rules (for example the Maharashtra Civil Services (Conduct) Rules 1979); **PSU employees** their company's Conduct, Discipline and Appeal Rules; **academics** their university or UGC/AICTE norms.

**Practical rule:** no proposal, fee negotiation, engagement or use of a firm name until written sanction is received. Keep the sanction letter on file.

### Permission request (template)

> To: [Competent authority, through proper channel]
> Subject: Request for previous sanction under Rule 15 of the CCS (Conduct) Rules 1964
>
> 1. Name, designation, office.
> 2. Nature of proposed activity: [training / advisory / authorship / lecture], for [organisation], [paid / honorary].
> 3. Duration and time commitment: [dates]; to be undertaken outside office hours and without use of official resources.
> 4. Expected fee or honorarium: ₹[amount]; I undertake to comply with FR 46 and any deposit requirement.
> 5. Declaration: the activity has no connection with my official duties; the organisation has no official dealings with my office [or details]; no official information will be used (Rule 11); there is no conflict of interest.
> 6. Request: previous sanction may kindly be accorded.
>
> Signature, date.

## 2. Professional bodies (check current rules)

| Professional | Source | Points relevant to advisory work |
|---|---|---|
| Advocate | Advocates Act 1961; Bar Council of India Rules, Part VI Chapter II | Restrictions on soliciting work and advertising (Rule 36, as amended); a practising advocate shall not personally engage in business, though may be a sleeping partner (Rule 47), may be a non-executive director but not a managing director or secretary of a company (Rule 48), and shall not be a full-time salaried employee while practising (Rule 49); duty of confidentiality; privilege under BSA 2023 |
| Chartered accountant | Chartered Accountants Act 1949; ICAI Code of Ethics | Independence for assurance work; restrictions on other occupations; confidentiality; non-solicitation of clients |
| Company secretary / cost accountant | ICSI / ICMAI codes | Similar independence and confidentiality duties |
| ISACA certified (CISA, CISM, CRISC, CGEIT) | ISACA Code of Professional Ethics | Confidentiality; objectivity; competence; inform stakeholders of results |
| ISC2 certified (CISSP and others) | ISC2 Code of Ethics | Protect society; act honourably and legally; provide competent service; advance the profession |
| CERT-In empanelled auditor | CERT-In empanelment guidelines and undertakings | Non-disclosure, data handling, conflict-of-interest rules, conduct of audits from India |
| Expert witness | Court rules; BSA 2023 (expert opinion) | Overriding duty to the court; disclose prior roles |

## 3. Private-sector employees

- Check the employment contract for moonlighting, outside-work approval, IP assignment (including work done outside hours), confidentiality and conflict-of-interest policies.
- Dual employment is a disciplinary matter, and a post-employment non-compete is generally void under s.27 (see `nda-review-checklist.md`), but confidentiality survives.

## 4. Gifts, hospitality and inducements

- Follow the firm's and the client's gifts policy; record and decline anything above the threshold.
- Never accept anything from a party to a matter you are assessing or investigating.
- Public-sector clients: anti-corruption laws (Prevention of Corruption Act 1988) and integrity pacts apply; for foreign touchpoints, consider the UK Bribery Act 2010 and US FCPA.

## 5. Ethics escalation

1. Raise the concern with the engagement lead.
2. If unresolved, escalate to the firm's risk or ethics lead.
3. Record the concern, the advice and the decision.
4. Where law or a professional code requires reporting, seek counsel on the duty and its route.
5. Never retaliate against a person who raises a concern in good faith.
